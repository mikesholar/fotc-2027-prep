# 5-Day Hypertrophy Program — Free Weights Only

**Goal:** maximize muscle gain
**Equipment:** barbell + plates, dumbbells, bench, rack. (Pull-up bar assumed — substitutions listed if you don't have one.)
**Structure:** 4 weeks of building volume, then a deload. Repeat.

---

## Weekly Schedule

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Mon | Lower A — squat emphasis |
| Tue | Upper A — push emphasis |
| Wed | Off (walk, mobility) |
| Thu | Upper B — pull emphasis |
| Fri | Lower B — hinge emphasis |
| Sat | Upper C — delts, arms, weak points |
| Sun | Off |

If Sat doesn't work, run Mon/Tue/Wed off/Thu/Fri/Sat off/Sun. Just keep one rest day between the two upper days early in the week.

## Day 1 — Lower A (Squat Emphasis)

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Back Squat | 4 × 5–8 | Main lift. Leave 1–2 reps in reserve until week 4. |
| Bulgarian Split Squat (DB) | 3 × 8–12 /leg | Rear foot on bench. Brutal, don't skip. |
| Romanian Deadlift | 3 × 8–10 | Push hips back, stretch the hamstring, don't round. |
| DB Walking Lunge | 3 × 10–12 /leg | Or reverse lunge if space is tight. |
| Standing Calf Raise | 4 × 10–15 | Barbell on back or DB in hand, toes on a plate. |
| Weighted Sit-up / Hanging Leg Raise | 3 × 10–15 | |

## Day 2 — Upper A (Push Emphasis)

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Barbell Bench Press | 4 × 5–8 | Main lift. |
| Standing Overhead Press | 3 × 6–10 | Strict. No leg drive. |
| Incline DB Press | 3 × 8–12 | ~30° bench angle. |
| DB Lateral Raise | 4 × 12–20 | Light, slow, high reps. Side delts respond to volume. |
| DB Flye or Deficit Push-up | 3 × 10–15 | Deep stretch at the bottom. |
| Overhead Triceps Extension (DB/EZ) | 3 × 10–15 | Overhead position loads the long head. |

---

## Weekly Volume Check

Roughly, per week: chest 14 sets, back 18, quads 13, hamstrings 13, side delts 8+, biceps 6 direct, triceps 6 direct. That's in the productive range (10–20 sets per muscle) without being so much you can't recover.

---

## How to Progress

**Within a set:** stop 1–2 reps short of failure for weeks 1–3. Week 4, take the last set of each exercise to 0–1 reps left.

**Week to week (double progression):**
1. Hit the bottom of the rep range on all sets with a given weight.
2. Next session, add reps — same weight, more reps.
3. When you hit the top of the range on every set, add weight: +5 lb upper body, +10 lb lower body. Reps reset to the bottom of the range.

**Across the mesocycle:**

| Week | Approach |
|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline. 2 reps in reserve. Bottom of rep ranges. |
| 2 | Add reps. Add 1 set to 2–3 lagging exercises. |
| 3 | Add reps or load. Push closer to failure. |
| 4 | Peak week. 0–1 reps in reserve on final sets. |
| 5 | **Deload:** half the sets, ~60% of the weight, nowhere near failure. |

Then start week 1 again with heavier baseline weights.

---

## The Part That Actually Determines Results

Training is maybe 40% of this. The rest:

**Calories.** You need a surplus. Roughly +250–400 above maintenance. Target **0.5–0.75 lb of bodyweight gain per week**. Faster than that and you're mostly adding fat. If the scale hasn't moved in two weeks, add 200 calories.

**Protein.** ~0.8–1.0 g per pound of bodyweight, daily, spread across 4+ meals. This is the single most-cheated variable and the one that matters most.

**Sleep.** 7–9 hours. Chronic short sleep measurably blunts muscle gain and raises injury risk. There's no supplement that compensates.

**Creatine monohydrate.** 5 g/day, any time. Cheap, well-studied, and one of the few supplements with real evidence behind it.

**Track your lifts.** Write down weight and reps every session. If the numbers aren't climbing over a month, something upstream (food or sleep) is the problem, not the program.

## Substitutions

| Missing | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Pull-up bar | DB pullover + extra rowing volume |
| Bench | Floor press, DB floor press, hip thrust off a couch/step |
| Leg curl machine | Nordic curls, single-leg RDL, sliding leg curls on a towel |
| Lat pulldown | Pull-ups, pullovers, chest-supported rows |
| Cable lateral raise | Higher-rep DB laterals, lean-away single-arm laterals |

---

*Realistic expectation: a lifter with a year or two of training who eats and sleeps properly can add roughly 10–20 lb of muscle in a year. Newer lifters gain faster, experienced lifters slower. Consistency across months beats optimization of any single session.*
